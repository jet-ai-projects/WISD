TL;DRSelf-distillation usually builds a stronger self-teacher by giving the model privileged information, such as reference solutions or environment feedback. WISD reverses the pair: the original model is the stronger member, and a copy weakened by low-bit quantization or magnitude pruning is the weaker one. Their token-level log-probability contrast supervises the model on its own rollouts, from unlabeled prompts alone. Across five reasoning models and five math and code benchmarks, it improves average accuracy by 4.0 points with quantization and 5.5 with pruning.

Abstract

Self-distillation offers a way to improve reasoning models when a suitable external teacher is unavailable. However, common approaches rely on privileged information to construct a stronger self-teacher, limiting their use when only unlabeled prompts are available. In this paper, we introduce Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation (WISD), which instead constructs a weaker counterpart of the original model and uses the contrast between the two as a training signal without requiring privileged information. We explore two ways to construct the weaker model: low-bit quantization and magnitude pruning. We evaluate WISD across diverse architectures and scales on mathematical and coding reasoning tasks. Across these benchmarks, quantization and pruning improve average accuracy over the original models by 4.0 and 5.5 percentage points, respectively, achieving state-of-the-art performance even without privileged information. Notably, pruning raises Qwen3.5-4B's accuracy from 57.7% to 70.5% on HMMT 2025 and from 39.1% to 56.7% on LiveCodeBench v6. Quantization raises Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's accuracy from 68.5% to 74.6% and from 61.8% to 69.5% on the same benchmarks, respectively. These results establish WISD as a novel and effective self-distillation framework for large reasoning models.

01 · Method

Don't strengthen the teacher. Weaken a copy.

A pair of models carries a training signal in the difference between them, even when neither is stronger than the student. Prior self-distillation builds the stronger member with extra context. WISD keeps the model itself as the stronger member and builds the weaker one from its own weights.

1

Reverse the pair

Viewed as a pair, prior self-distillation sets the model itself as the weaker member π− and the model with privileged context c as the stronger one π+. WISD sets π+ = π0, the original model, and π− = πw, its weakened copy.

\[ r_w(a\mid s)=\log\pi_0(a\mid s)-\log\pi_w(a\mid s) \]
2

Weaken without data

Per group of 128 input columns with maximum MG: round each weight to b = 3 bits, or drop weights at or below τMG with τ = 1/6 and restore each row's norm. Embeddings, heads, norms and routers are untouched; only the frozen scorer is weakened.

\[ \widehat W_{ij}=\Delta_G\,\mathrm{clip}\!\Big(\mathrm{round}\big(\tfrac{W_{ij}}{\Delta_G}\big),-q_b,q_b\Big),\quad \Delta_G=\tfrac{M_G}{q_b} \]
3

Distill the contrast

Following the offline DOPD procedure of Lightning Weave, the contrast tilts the original model into an explicit target, and the student minimizes its KL to it. Tokens the original prefers over its weakened copy gain probability.

\[ q_w(a\mid s)\propto\pi_0(a\mid s)\exp\!\big(r_w(a\mid s)/\alpha\big) \]\[ \mathcal L(\theta)=\alpha\,\mathbb E_{s\sim d_{\pi_0}}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\,\|\,q_w)\big] \]
4

A small, label-free budget

Per domain: 3,200 unlabeled prompts, four responses each, 12,800 cached trajectories and 200 optimizer updates. The frozen pair scores the cache once, so no scoring model is live during training.

03 · Ablation

Weaken moderately: not too little, not too much.

Too little weakening gives too little contrast. Too much destroys the weak model's reasoning, and noise drowns the signal. Both constructions peak in between, and the best settings also shorten responses.
